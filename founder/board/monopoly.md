# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market that could be owned.** "Small teams who run several coding agents overnight, on their own machines and logins" is narrow enough to dominate first. Nobody in this brief is named as owning it.
- **A real wedge, used by its builder.** Two weeks of nightly real use proves the founder has the pain. The engine owns order, retries, loop limits, stop time and usage-limit waits. That is know-how scripts do not give you.
- **Vendor-neutral by design.** One flow mixes Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode and local models. A model vendor will not build this, because each vendor wants you on its own agent. That neutrality is the most defensible position in the brief.
- **Seeds of switching cost.** Flows, run history, gates and Spaces pile up per team. Drawn pipelines become the team's process, and process is sticky.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The agent vendors absorb the pipeline.** Claude Code, Codex and their peers add scheduling, background runs, retries and review queues to their own products. For a team on one vendor, "good enough and built in" beats "10x better and separate". Night Shift then keeps only the multi-vendor niche, and the brief has no number for how large that niche is.
2. **No secret, no 10x, open source.** The brief names no proprietary technology. The product is a careful assembly of known parts (canvas, scheduler, CLI calls). Apache-2.0 lets anyone fork it. A funded workflow or agent-ops tool could copy the visual flow in month six. Today the advantage is effort and taste, not a moat.
3. **No distribution.** One user, no waitlist, no pitch, one part-time builder. The brief has no path to the first 100 teams and no cost per team. A good product with no way to reach buyers is not a business.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who else sells pipeline orchestration for coding agents to a 2–15 person team? What do they charge? (/founder-competitors)
- What is the secret? What do you know about overnight agent work that the vendors and competitors have not seen?
- What part of Night Shift is 10x better than a script plus cron plus a vendor's background mode? Which number shows it (merged PRs per night, rescue rate, hours saved)?
- How many teams already run agents overnight, and how many use two or more agent vendors? (/founder-consumer)
- How do the first 100 teams arrive, and what does each one cost? (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- Which durable advantage do you plan to build: a network effect (shared flows and kits between teams), a brand, or a data asset (what works, per model and per task)?
- If the open-source project wins adoption, what do teams pay for that a fork cannot give them? (/founder-cfo)
- Why now, and will the position still hold in ten years if agents get good enough to need no pipeline?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Name the small market and own it.** Pick one segment (for example, agencies on two or more agent vendors). Show a credible path to most of that segment. Size it with /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer.
2. **Prove a 10x number.** Show one measured result against the script-plus-vendor default, from users other than the founder.
3. **Pick one durable advantage and start it now.** The obvious candidate is a shared library of flows and kits that gets better with each team (a network effect). Vendor neutrality on its own is a position, not a moat.
4. **Show distribution.** Ship the pitch. Get 10 outside teams running a nightly flow, with a known cost per team, before you think about a paid tier.
