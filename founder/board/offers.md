# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The dream outcome is vivid.** "You wake up to a stack of pull requests and a short list of decisions" is a concrete outcome a buyer can picture. Few dev tools state their outcome this clearly.
- **The buyer segment has money and is reachable.** Small agencies and startups already pay for coding-agent subscriptions. They live on GitHub, where the product already sits. Whether the segment is large is an open question.
- **Time delay is short once it works.** A flow runs tonight and the result is there in the morning. That is a fast feedback loop for a dev tool.
- **The stack is already wide.** Retries, rescue by a stronger model, usage-limit waits, gates on the phone, per-run cost, shared Spaces. Many of the buyer's obstacles on the way to the outcome already have a solution.
- **No resale of model access.** The cost to deliver stays low, so a paid layer could carry high margins.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood has no proof.** One user, two weeks, no outside evidence. A team lead has no reason to believe that unattended agents give mergeable PRs on *their* code. In this lens, low perceived likelihood is the usual cause of death. One founder's nights do not move it.
2. **Effort and sacrifice are high.** The buyer must self-host a Host, pair computers, draw a flow, set up agent logins, and accept pre-alpha risk on macOS or Linux only. The outcome sits behind a lot of setup. The alternative is a script they already have, which costs nothing to keep.
3. **There is no offer, so the free version wins by default.** It is Apache-2.0 with no price and no paid layer. Without a named offer, the only lever is "free", and that makes it a commodity next to scripts and other tools. Who these competitors are and what they charge is unknown (/founder-competitors).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which 5 teams outside your own have this pain this month? Have they tried overnight agent runs and given up? (/founder-consumer)
- What share of overnight PRs merge without rework? A number like this is the proof that lifts likelihood.
- How long does a new team take from download to the first useful morning? What can you cut from that path?
- What would a team pay to skip the self-hosting? A hosted Host, a done-for-you first flow, a support line? (/founder-cfo for price and margin)
- What could you guarantee? For example: "a merged PR in the first week, or we build your flow for you."
- If you doubled the price of the paid layer, would you still be the obvious choice? Against what?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Proof from others.** At least 5 outside teams run real nightly flows for 4 weeks. Track merged-PR rate and time saved per team.
2. **First result in one evening.** A new team gets its first useful morning from a starter flow, with no flow drawing and no Host setup.
3. **A named paid offer on top of the open source.** For example, a hosted Host plus a done-for-you first flow plus a guarantee. Test the price with real teams before you build more node types.
4. **Market numbers.** Size of the segment and willingness to pay come from /founder-consumer and /founder-cfo, not from guesses.

Until conditions 1 and 2 hold, more features do not raise the value of the offer. Proof and less effort do.
